> This is one of the [paradoxes of time](../).

Unlike the [grandfather paradox](../grandfather/), the predestination paradox creates no contradiction; instead it creates a **closed causal loop**: the future returns to the past and becomes the reason the past is the way it is. Oedipus leaves home to escape the oracle that he will “kill his father and marry his mother”, and precisely by doing so walks into its fulfilment — this is its oldest archetype.

The difficulty is not “will there be a contradiction”, but **free will**: if every act of resistance you make is already written into settled history, do you have any room left for a real “choice”?

Historically, people mostly chewed on it in philosophy and literature (Greek tragedy, fatalism). In physics, it is in fact a **positive example** of the Novikov self-consistency principle: it is precisely a self-consistent history, so it is allowed to exist, with no logical problem at all.

Its “answer”, therefore, is not to dissolve the paradox but to accept a picture: **self-consistent causal loops are allowed**.

The questions it raises are pointed — a world that is perfectly consistent logically may at the same time be a world with no “room for rewriting”. The age-old tension between determinism and free will is amplified to the maximum here by time travel.
