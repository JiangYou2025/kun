> This is one of the [paradoxes of time](../).

The **germ** of this thought experiment goes back to Einstein in 1907 and Tolman in 1917: both noticed that if signals could travel faster than light, one could send telegrams into the past. The word “tachyon” was coined by Feinberg in 1967 for hypothetical particles faster than light, while the vivid name “tachyonic antitelephone” comes from the 1970 paper of that title by Benford, Book and Newcomb. It is not about actually building such a telephone, but a **proof by contradiction**: suppose faster-than-light signals exist — what follows?

The difficulty comes from the core of relativity: when a signal travels faster than light, the order of events becomes **frame-dependent** — in one frame A precedes B, in another B precedes A. This is really an extension of the “relativity of simultaneity” in the [ladder/barn paradox](../ladder-barn/): since “which comes first” already varies from observer to observer, once a signal is fast enough to “take a shortcut” ahead of light, for some observers it really is reversed. For lightlike and timelike causal signals this is no problem, but with faster-than-light signals you can piece together “an effect earlier than its cause”.

Physicists' response was not to patch it up but to use it as a **criterion**: since allowing faster-than-light signalling would make it possible to send messages into the past and thereby break causality (you could stop yourself from sending a signal you have already sent), the only possible conclusion is

$$v_{\text{information}} \le c.$$

**Causality forbids any information from propagating faster than light.** Note that what it forbids is “information / causal influence”, not every “faster-than-light phenomenon”: phase velocities, the correlations of quantum entanglement and the expansion of the universe can all be “faster than light”, but none of them can be used to transmit a controllable signal.

The significance of the antitelephone is that it raises the “speed-of-light limit” from an empirical regularity to a **logical requirement for preserving causal order**: not “it just so happens that nothing is faster than light”, but “nothing that can carry causation can possibly be faster than light”.
