> This is one of the [paradoxes of time](../).

In 1908, in *The Unreality of Time*, the British philosopher McTaggart divided time into two series: the **A-series** (past–present–future, which flows and changes) and the **B-series** (earlier–later, a fixed relation).

His puzzle (which is also his argument) runs as follows: change is the essence of time, and change can only be supplied by the A-series (in the B-series, “1066 is earlier than 2026” is true forever; nothing changes). But the A-series is itself contradictory: one and the same event must be “past”, “present” and “future”, and these three are mutually exclusive. Defend it by saying “it is first future and only later present”, and you must presuppose a second layer of time, falling into an infinite regress.

Later philosophers have not agreed on a “solution”; instead they split into camps, each holding its ground. **A-theorists** insist that time really flows and that the present is special (presentism); **B-theorists** hold that time, like space, forms a single “block universe” in which past, present and future are equally real, and so-called “passage” is only an illusion of consciousness — a view that fits neatly with the spacetime picture of relativity.

McTaggart's own conclusion was radical: **time is not real.**

Most people do not accept this radical conclusion, but the opposition he forced into the open — whether time really flows, or whether everything is already “laid out” in four-dimensional spacetime — remains the central battlefield of the philosophy of time, and directly echoes the debate in physics over “whether time is fundamental”.
