## 10.1 Why we need the Transformer

RNNs/LSTMs pass information along step by step, so the more distant a dependency, the more easily it fades in transit, and they **cannot be parallelized** (they must compute one step at a time). The Transformer's motivation is to use **attention** to get there in one step: let any two time steps **interact directly**, however far apart, while the whole sequence is computed in parallel. It first sparked a revolution in NLP and was then carried over to time series, giving rise to a string of models — and to an unexpected lesson.

## 10.2 The self-attention mechanism

$$\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V.$$

Each time step uses its own query to compute similarities with the keys of all time steps, then uses them to take a weighted sum of the values — so it can **directly see** relevant moments arbitrarily far away. Capturing long-range dependencies efficiently and flexibly is its biggest selling point.

## 10.3 Positional encoding: putting "order" back in

Attention by itself is "blind" to order (shuffling the time steps leaves the result unchanged), so **positional encodings** must be injected separately to tell the model what comes first. Common approaches: sinusoidal encoding, learnable encoding, rotary positional embedding (RoPE).

## 10.4 The evolution of time series Transformers

| Model | Year | Key innovation |
|------|------|---------|
| Informer | 2021 | ProbSparse attention, reducing the $O(L^2)$ complexity |
| Autoformer | 2021 | Replaces standard attention with an auto-correlation mechanism |
| FEDformer | 2022 | Frequency-enhanced attention |
| DLinear | 2022 | A single linear layer beats complex Transformers (a wake-up call) |
| PatchTST | 2023 | Splits the series into patches as tokens + channel independence |
| iTransformer | 2024 | Treats variables (rather than time steps) as tokens |
| TimeMixer | 2024 | Multi-scale mixing, a return to the lightweight MLP route |

## 10.5 The lesson of DLinear: complex ≠ better

In 2022 the DLinear paper showed that a **single-layer linear model** (first decomposing trend and seasonality, then mapping linearly) **outperformed every previous Transformer variant** on several long-sequence benchmarks. This wake-up call forced the whole community to reflect — many attention mechanisms had learned nothing more on time series than a simple linear mapping. It also echoes what we have stressed again and again since Lecture 3: **beat the linear baseline first, then talk about complexity**.

## 10.6 PatchTST: patching + channel independence

PatchTST won back ground with two simple yet effective design choices:

1. **Patching**: group several consecutive time steps into a patch (much as ViT splits an image into patches), reducing the number of tokens while preserving local semantics.
2. **Channel independence**: each variable is processed on its own, without forcibly mixing channels.

This lean design set new records on several benchmarks in 2023, showing that "using the right structure" matters more than "piling on attention".

## 10.7 Should you use a Transformer?

- **Complexity**: standard self-attention is $O(L^2)$, expensive for long sequences — exactly what Informer and others tried to optimize, and the motivation for KUN's hierarchical approach in the next lecture.
- **Data requirements**: many parameters and a big appetite; on small data they are often beaten by simple models like DLinear.
- **Rule of thumb**: consider them first for **long sequences, many variables and massive data**; otherwise try linear / classical baselines first. The value of attention must be proven by comparison with baselines.
