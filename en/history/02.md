AR and MA had each been invented, but there was no unifying theory: exactly which processes can they represent? At the same time, anti-aircraft fire control in the Second World War turned "predicting a target's future position from a noisy signal" into an urgent engineering problem. In this decade, mathematicians and engineers laid two cornerstones.

## Key contribution 1: the Wold decomposition (1938)

What is the intrinsic structure of an arbitrary (covariance-)stationary process? Are AR and MA special cases, or a universal law?

Herman Wold proved that **any** covariance-stationary process can be uniquely decomposed into a deterministic component and an infinite-order moving average:

$$ X_t = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_j\,\varepsilon_{t-j} + V_t,\qquad \psi_0=1,\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_j^2<\infty $$

$$\varepsilon_t$$ is the innovation (the part of the series that is **linearly unpredictable**), $$\sum\psi_j\varepsilon_{t-j}$$ is its linearly predictable stochastic part, and $$V_t$$ is the deterministic component that can be **perfectly predicted** from the infinitely distant past. This shows that **the MA($$\infty$$) representation is universal**; under invertibility conditions, AR and MA can be converted into one another (AR($$p$$) $$\Leftrightarrow$$ MA($$\infty$$)). Square-summability of the coefficients, $$\sum\psi_j^2<\infty$$, guarantees a finite variance.

It provides the **existence foundation** for the whole of linear time series theory: every ARMA model is simply an approximation of this set of Wold coefficients.

## Key contribution 2: Wiener–Kolmogorov optimal prediction (1940s)

Given the entire history, what makes a prediction the "best" one?

Wiener (in continuous time) and Kolmogorov (in discrete time) independently proved that the minimum mean-squared-error prediction is the **conditional expectation**, which is equivalent to **projecting** the target onto the (linear) space spanned by the history. Using the Wold coefficients, the $$h$$-step prediction is:

$$ \hat X_{t+h} = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j+h}\,\varepsilon_{t-j} $$

with prediction error variance:

$$ \mathrm{Var}(X_{t+h}-\hat X_{t+h}) = \sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\psi_j^2 $$

The prediction keeps only the influence of "shocks that have already happened" (indices $$\le t$$) and discards unknown future shocks. The error variance **increases monotonically** with the horizon $$h$$ — a quantitative explanation of why "the further ahead, the less accurate": the error accumulates more and more unforeseeable innovations $$\varepsilon_{t+1},\dots,\varepsilon_{t+h}$$. As $$h\to\infty$$, the variance tends to the unconditional variance of the process, and the prediction degenerates to the mean.

Prediction was made rigorous as a problem of **geometric projection**; the Wiener filter was put to practical use in fire control and communications, turning mathematical theory directly into battlefield and engineering capability.

The Wold decomposition plus optimal projection prediction form the classical paradigm of "first model the Wold structure, then predict by projection" — the theoretical prelude to the Box–Jenkins methodology.
