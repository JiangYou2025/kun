> This is one entry in [the nature of time](../).

In 1927 Heidegger published *Being and Time*. Physics had just geometrized and objectified time, while phenomenology (Husserl) had turned toward the inner consciousness of time. Heidegger wanted to ask something more fundamental: not “what thing is time?” but “how does time belong to our **being**?”

His method was a phenomenological analysis of the human way of being — what he called **“Dasein”**. His conclusion: time is not a container we happen to sit inside; it is the very **structure** of Dasein's being. Dasein is always “ahead of itself”, projecting toward the future, carrying its past, dealing with the present; above all it exists as **being-toward-death** — the awareness of its own finitude makes the “future” the most authentic dimension of time. Temporality is the meaning of Dasein's being.

*Being and Time* is one of the most influential philosophical works of the twentieth century; it reshaped existentialism, hermeneutics and phenomenology. It turned time completely around, from “an object to be measured” into “the way being unfolds” — clock time becomes merely a derivative, impoverished form of this primordial temporality.

Heidegger stands at the far end from “data time”: for him the most authentic time is not on the horizontal axis but in a mortal being's projects and cares about the future. He reminds us that behind every sequence we analyse there is a mortal human being who has a reason **to want to predict the future**.
