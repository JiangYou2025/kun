> This is one of the [paradoxes of time](../).

The bootstrap paradox (“bootstrap” from “pulling yourself up by your own bootstraps”) is another form of causal loop: what circulates in the loop is not a chain of causes, but **the information or the object itself**, and that thing **has no origin**.

Stories of “things with no provenance” were first pushed to their limit by science-fiction writers. In the 1941 short story *By His Bootstraps*, and again in 1959 in *—All You Zombies—*, Robert A. Heinlein packed objects and even a person's entire ancestry into such loops: the protagonist ends up being both his own father and his own mother, the whole bloodline closing in on itself. The philosopher David Lewis later made a point of noting that such stories are **perfectly consistent** logically — unlike the grandfather paradox they do not contradict themselves; the only trouble is that the “provenance” vanishes into thin air.

The difficulty is not contradiction — the whole loop can be entirely consistent — but a deeper question: **can information exist out of nothing?** Every note of the score is there, yet no act of creation ever produced it; its “composition” has been quietly replaced by “copying”.

This question still has no satisfying answer. It violates no logic, but it badly offends intuition: we believe that anything complex and ordered must come from somewhere, and the bootstrap paradox offers a counterexample — a pattern that sustains itself on a **closed timelike curve** (a path where time bends into a loop and the end joins back to the beginning), yet arises from nothing.

Its value lies precisely in turning attention to **information and origins**: can complexity exist without evolution or creation, purely by virtue of a self-consistent loop? It echoes the thermodynamic question of “where order comes from” — only this time, time has been bent into a circle.
