> This is one entry in [the nature of time](../).

In the second half of the twentieth century, the holy grail of physics was to unify general relativity and quantum mechanics into “quantum gravity”. But the two theories take diametrically opposed attitudes toward time: quantum mechanics needs an external, uniformly flowing time in which to write evolution, while in general relativity time is dynamical, curvable and without any privileged status.

In the 1960s, Wheeler and DeWitt tried to write down a quantum equation for the universe as a whole. Their method was to extend the Schrödinger equation of quantum mechanics to the entire universe. The result was astonishing: the resulting **Wheeler–DeWitt equation** contains no time variable at all — it takes the form $\hat{H}\,\Psi=0$, as if saying that the wave function of the whole universe is “frozen” and does not evolve in any time. This became known as physics' **“problem of time”**.

The puzzle still has no accepted solution, but it has given rise to deep conjectures: perhaps at the most fundamental level time is **not a fundamental quantity** at all, but **emerges** from a deeper, timeless structure — the way temperature emerges from molecular motion. Time may not be the stage of the universe, but an effect performed on that stage.

This is the most radical question one can put to “time series”: if even fundamental physics may contain no “time”, could the horizontal axis along which we line up our data be only the macroscopic shadow of some deeper order? The time we use so effortlessly may not lie at the bottom of the world.
