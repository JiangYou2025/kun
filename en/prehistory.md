## Prehistory: before the 19th century

[The main history timeline](../history/#timeline) begins in 1900 with the theory of stochastic processes, but humanity has been “reading time series” for far longer. That history emerged in three successive strands: first **timekeeping and calendars, born to measure time** (sundials, water clocks, calendars); then **a long accumulation of observations** (long-term records in astronomy, hydrology, population and prices); and finally, only in the 17th–19th centuries, **naive methods** (life tables, line charts, harmonic analysis, least squares, moving averages), which joined modern spectral analysis in 1898 with Schuster's **periodogram**. The “Category” column in the table below tells the three apart.

| Date | Category | Person / civilization | Exploration (scattered data or methods) |
|------|------|------------|--------------------------|
| ~3000 BC | Timekeeping · calendar | Ancient Egypt / Mesopotamia | **Sundials, water clocks, lunisolar calendars**: cutting time into measurable units |
| ~3000–2000 BC | Observation | Stonehenge | Megaliths aligned on the solstices, tracking the annual return of the Sun |
| ~1600 BC | Observation | Babylon | **Astronomical diaries**: systematic records of planetary positions and eclipses (calendars and celestial prediction) |
| Since antiquity | Observation | Ancient Egypt | **The Nilometer**: year-by-year records of flood levels, an environmental series spanning millennia |
| 800 BC– | Observation | Chinese court historians | Continuous records of **sunspots, comets, solar eclipses and earthquakes** — among the longest observational series in existence |
| ~150 BC | Method | Hipparchus | Comparing observations centuries apart, discovers the **precession of the equinoxes**; compiles a star catalogue |
| ~100 BC | Timekeeping · method | The Antikythera mechanism | An “ancient computer” that modelled the heavens with gears and predicted eclipses |
| ~150 | Method | Ptolemy | The *Almagest*'s **epicycle model**: superposed periods to predict planetary motion |
| ~800–900 | Observation | Maya | **The Dresden Codex**: Venus and eclipse tables, the Long Count calendar |
| 1088 | Timekeeping | Su Song (astronomical clock tower) | A water-driven Chinese astronomical clock: continuous time-telling and celestial display |
| 1213 | Method | Medieval Europe | **The London Bridge tide table**: one of the earliest tide-prediction tables |
| 1582 | Timekeeping · calendar | The Gregorian calendar | Corrects calendar drift and unifies the long-term time scale |
| 1580s | Observation | Tycho Brahe | Decades of **precise, continuous** observations of planetary positions |
| 1609 | Method | Kepler | Derives the three laws of planetary motion from Tycho's decades-long **series of Mars positions** |
| 1610 | Observation | Galileo | **Records the positions of Jupiter's four moons night after night** — a classic early time-series dataset; also observes moving sunspots and times with the pendulum |
| 1656 | Timekeeping | Huygens | **The pendulum clock**: improves timekeeping accuracy by an order of magnitude |
| 1662 | Method | John Graunt | *Natural and Political Observations … upon the Bills of Mortality*: the first **life table**, analysing London's series of deaths |
| 1693/1705 | Method | Halley | Life table; and, from historical records, predicts the return period of **Halley's comet** |
| 1786 | Method | William Playfair | Invents the **line chart**: data drawn for the first time as a “curve changing over time” |
| 1807/1822 | Method | Fourier | **Harmonic analysis**: decomposing a periodic signal into a sum of sine waves |
| 1805/1809 | Method | Legendre · Gauss | **Least squares**: fitting celestial orbits to observation series (Ceres) |
| 1835 | Method | Quetelet | “**Social physics**”: applying statistical averages to social and population series |
| 1843 | Observation · method | Schwabe | Discovers the roughly **11-year sunspot cycle** |
| 1847 | Method | Buys-Ballot | **Period-detection tables**: searching a series for hidden periods |
| 1848– | Observation | Wolf | The **Wolf sunspot number** series (later exactly what Yule modelled) |
| 1854 | Method · application | John Snow · William Farr | **Time-ordered statistics** of cholera and deaths, the embryo of public-health series analysis |
| 1862/1875 | Method | Jevons · Juglar | **Business cycles**, commercial fluctuations, and even the sunspot–business-cycle hypothesis |
| 1876 | Method · computation | Kelvin (W. Thomson) | **The tide-predicting machine**: mechanically synthesizing tide series by harmonic analysis |
| 1884 | Method | Poynting | Uses **moving averages** to smooth price series and strip out the trend |
| 1886 | Method | Galton | **Regression** and “regression to the mean” |
| 1898 | Method | Schuster | Invents the **periodogram**: testing for hidden periods — leading straight to 20th-century spectral analysis |

> The logic of the prehistory is **observation first, methods scattered**: timekeeping and calendars first quantified time; long records in astronomy, hydrology, population and prices then accumulated data; and finally harmonic analysis, least squares, moving averages and the periodogram supplied the earliest tools. After 1900 these were unified by the theory of stochastic processes, formally opening [the main history timeline](../history/#timeline).
