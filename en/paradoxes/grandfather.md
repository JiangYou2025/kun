> This is one of the [paradoxes of time](../).

This is the classic logic bomb of time-travel stories, pushing “changing the past” straight into a self-cancelling dead end.

The difficulty is a closed self-reference: your existence depends on some fact about the past, and your going back into the past negates exactly that fact.

Responses to it split into several lines. The bluntest simply denies that going back into the past is possible at all (see the [chronology protection conjecture](../chronology-protection/)). But physics also offers ways out that “allow time travel without self-contradiction”.

The first is the **Novikov self-consistency principle**, proposed in the 1980s by the Russian astrophysicist Igor Novikov: physics only allows histories that are **self-consistent** to happen. Once you are back in the past, however you try, you will “just happen” to fail — the gun jams, you kill the wrong man, it turns out he was not your grandfather after all. The universe does not allow contradictory sequences of events; probability automatically squeezes inconsistent branches down to zero. The second is the **many-worlds** view: the grandfather you kill belongs to some branch universe, while the branch you came from is left untouched, so there is no contradiction, only a fork.

The value of the grandfather paradox is that it dragged “time travel” out of science fiction and into serious discussion, and forced out a criterion: **any physics that allows travel into the past must either guarantee that history is self-consistent or allow the world to branch** — otherwise causality collapses.
