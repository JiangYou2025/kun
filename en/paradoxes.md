## Closing: paradox as a method

> For most paradoxes, the “solution” does not make the problem go away; it **clarifies concepts**. Zeno gave rise to limits and calculus; the reversibility paradox forced out statistical mechanics and the arrow of time; Maxwell's demon brought information into physics and measured the lower bound on the energy cost of computation; the grandfather paradox drove “time travel” into the corner of self-consistent causality or many worlds; and Newcomb's paradox squeezed free will versus determinism into a multiple-choice question with money on the line. A time series merely lays data out along a time axis, but these paradoxes remind us that the “time axis” itself is far less self-evident than it looks.
