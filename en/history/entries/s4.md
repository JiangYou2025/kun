### Background

The [Transformer](../transformer/) dominated sequence modelling thanks to self-attention, but its $$O(n^2)$$ complexity is costly on **extremely long sequences** (audio and pixel sequences thousands of steps long, long-range dependency benchmarks); [RNN](../rnn/)-style models have linear complexity but struggle to remember distant information and train sequentially. Meanwhile, control theory had a mature language for describing continuous dynamics — the **linear state-space model (SSM)**, a close relative of the [Kalman filter](../kalman/). A natural question arose: could it be turned into a deep learning layer?

### The problem

Naively using a continuous SSM as a neural network layer runs into two major obstacles. The first is **memory**: a randomly initialized state matrix makes long-range information fade quickly, so distant dependencies cannot be captured. The second is **computation**: running the recursion step by step through time is both slow and prone to numerical instability. **Could there be a state-space layer that both remembers over the long term and trains efficiently in parallel?**

### Other approaches of the time (and their limits)

- **Transformer attention**: direct long-range interaction, but $$O(n^2)$$ memory and compute, which extremely long sequences cannot afford.
- **Gated RNNs ([LSTM](../lstm/)/GRU)**: linear complexity, but limited by vanishing gradients, making both long-range memory and parallel training difficult.
- **Linear / sparse attention**: reduces complexity, but often at the cost of poorer modelling of long-range dependencies.

### Method

Memory comes first. **HiPPO** theory gives a specific state matrix $$A$$ that makes the hidden state **compress the entire history online in an optimal way** (projecting the past onto a basis of orthogonal polynomials), so that it "remembers for a long time". Underneath is a continuous linear state space:

$$ x'(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t),\qquad y(t)=Cx(t)+Du(t) $$

Discretizing it with a step size (for example with the bilinear transform) gives the recursion $$x_k=\bar A x_{k-1}+\bar B u_k$$. The key insight of **S4** is that this linear recursion is equivalent to a single **long convolution**, with kernel

$$ \bar K=\big(C\bar B,\;C\bar A\bar B,\;C\bar A^2\bar B,\;\dots,\;C\bar A^{L-1}\bar B\big) $$

This yields a "dual mode": during training, the long convolution is computed with the FFT, in parallel over the whole sequence; during inference, the model returns to the recursion and steps forward in $$O(1)$$ like an RNN. To compute the powers of $$\bar A$$ efficiently, S4 uses a **structured (diagonal plus low-rank) parameterization** that brings the computation of the kernel down to near-linear complexity.

### Impact

On the Long Range Arena benchmark, designed specifically to test long-range dependencies, S4 led all kinds of efficient Transformers by a wide margin, and was the first to solve reasonably well its notoriously difficult Path-X task; it also performed outstandingly on tasks requiring extremely long context, such as raw audio and long-sequence image classification, combining linear complexity with strong memory.

### Significance and outlook

S4 brought the **state-space equations of control theory** back to the centre of sequence modelling, proving that "continuous dynamics + structured discretization + long convolution" is a powerful route beyond the Transformer. It directly inspired subsequent simplified and gated variants, and ultimately led to [Mamba](../mamba/), with its input-dependent (selective) parameters, which now rivals attention in long-sequence and high-throughput settings. From Kalman to S4, the thread of state spaces shines again across seventy years.
