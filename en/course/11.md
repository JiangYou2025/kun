## 11.1 Motivation: real series are "multi-scale"

Lecture 10 left us with two lessons: the Transformer's $O(L^2)$ is too expensive, and simple linear models are astonishingly strong. Is there a way to be **both efficient and able to capture rich structure**? The key observation is that real time series contain patterns at several scales simultaneously:

- **Short term**: hour-level fluctuations;
- **Medium term**: weekly / monthly trends;
- **Long term**: yearly seasonality.

A single-scale model (linear or Transformer alike) struggles to handle all scales at once. KUN's starting point is to use a **naturally hierarchical** structure to divide and conquer the different scales.

## 11.2 A precursor idea: N-BEATS

Before turning to KUN, look at **N-BEATS (2019)** — it showed that a purely feed-forward network can also forecast well:

- Each block outputs both a "backcast" (fitting the known) and a "forecast" (inferring the unknown).
- Multiple blocks are stacked, each processing the **residual** of the previous one, approaching the target step by step.
- The interpretable version forces the blocks to output trend / seasonal basis functions.

The idea of "residual stacking + division of labour" laid the groundwork for KUN's hierarchical design.

## 11.3 The core idea of Kernel U-Net

KUN brings the **U-Net** from the image domain to time series: **just as with images, first shrink to see the global outline, then enlarge to fill in local detail**.

1. **Encoder**: progressively downsamples, compressing a long sequence into a short latent representation — capturing coarser and coarser scales.
2. **Decoder**: progressively upsamples, restoring the original length — reconstructing from coarse to fine.
3. **Skip connections**: each encoder level connects to the corresponding decoder level — preserving the detail lost in compression.
4. **Pluggable kernels**: the down- / upsampling at each step can be linear, an MLP or attention — chosen as needed for flexible adaptation.

$$\text{Encoder: }\ (T, C) \xrightarrow{\text{chunk}} (T/k_1, C') \xrightarrow{\text{chunk}} \cdots \to (1, C_{\text{latent}})$$

$$\text{Decoder: }\ (1, C_{\text{latent}}) \xrightarrow{\text{expand}} \cdots \to (T, C)$$

## 11.4 Why a "U" shape

The image U-Net first shrinks the picture to see the "global outline", then enlarges it to fill in "local detail", with skip connections stitching the two ends together. Time series work the same way: **downsampling sees long-period trends, upsampling restores high-frequency detail** — a single structure naturally covers several time scales, without paying the $O(L^2)$ price for long-range dependencies the way a Transformer does (with a linear kernel the complexity is $O(T)$).

## 11.5 KUN compared with other models

| Property | DLinear | Transformer | KUN |
|------|---------|-------------|-----|
| Multi-scale | ✗ | Partial (patches) | ✓ Naturally hierarchical |
| Interpretability | Trend + seasonality | Attention maps | Each level corresponds to one scale |
| Flexible kernels | Linear | Attention | Linear / MLP / attention, selectable |
| Complexity | $O(T)$ | $O(T^2)$ | $O(T)$ (linear kernel) |
| Skip connections | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ Preserve detail |
