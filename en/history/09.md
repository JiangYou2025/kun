The explosion of deep learning in images and natural language, combined with the arrival of massive sequence data (retail SKUs, cloud monitoring, sensor networks), gave rise to a new paradigm: **global models** — a single network learning the shared patterns of thousands of series at once, instead of modelling each series separately.

## Key contribution 1: seq2seq and DeepAR's probabilistic forecasting

Single-series models cannot share information across series; and businesses often need **probabilistic forecasts** (quantiles, intervals) rather than a single point estimate.

Amazon's DeepAR (2017/2019) uses an autoregressive RNN that shares parameters across series and outputs the parameters of a likelihood distribution:

$$ \ell_t = \mathrm{RNN}(h_{t-1}, x_{t-1}, z_t),\qquad p(x_t\mid \cdot) = \mathcal N\!\big(\mu(\ell_t),\,\sigma(\ell_t)\big) $$

Training maximises the log-likelihood $$\sum_t \log p(x_t\mid\cdot)$$; at prediction time, the distribution is obtained by Monte Carlo **sampling of many future trajectories**.

The network no longer spits out a single number but the distribution parameters $$\mu,\sigma$$, so forecasts naturally carry uncertainty. Sharing across series lets sparse or newly launched (cold-start) series draw on "collective experience" — the core advantage of global models.

## Key contribution 2: attention and the Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017)

RNNs must compute sequentially, are hard to parallelise, and still struggle with long dependencies.

Self-attention lets any two positions in a sequence interact directly:

$$ \mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V $$

Each query $$Q$$ takes dot products with all keys $$K$$ to obtain similarities, which softmax normalises into weights used to form a weighted sum of the values $$V$$. Any two time steps are thus linked by a **direct path of length 1**, so long dependencies no longer need to be passed along step by step; and all positions can be computed **fully in parallel**. The scaling factor $$\sqrt{d_k}$$ prevents dot products from growing too large in high dimensions and pushing the softmax into its gradient-saturated region. The price is a computational and memory complexity of

$$ \mathcal O(L^2) $$

which **grows quadratically** with the sequence length $$L$$ — this became the main bottleneck for long-horizon forecasting and defined the direction of optimisation for the next decade.

**Other.** N-BEATS (2019), using pure feed-forward layers with residual stacking and interpretable trend/seasonality bases, showed that a deep model relying on neither recurrence nor attention could likewise win forecasting competitions.

Deep learning made "end-to-end, global, probabilistic" the mainstream, and attention became the common foundation of all later long-horizon Transformers.

The 2010s laid three cornerstones — global models, probabilistic forecasting and attention. They brought a leap in capability but also raised new problems ($$\mathcal O(L^2)$$ cost, possible over-complexity), leading directly to the debates and innovations of the 2020s.
